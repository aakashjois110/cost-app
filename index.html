    How it works

    Each condition is a list of steps, like a GP visit, an echo, a hospital stay or a medicine. Every step has a price, the % of patients who get it, and how many times they get it.

    Cost per patient = for each step, % of patients × number of times × price, all added up.

    Some steps only happen after another step. For example, if 60% of patients see a cardiologist and half of those get a CT, then 30% of all patients get a CT.

    Range: every number has a low and high guess. The page runs 600 random combinations of these and shows the middle 80% of results.

    Where the prices come from

    • MBS (doctors, tests, scans): Medicare pays the rebate in the MBS file (100% for GP visits, 85% for most other things out of hospital). The patient pays the rest plus any gap the doctor charges.
    • PBS (medicines): price per script worked out from the PBS files (cheapest brand plus wholesale, pharmacy and dispensing fees). I checked two against pbs.gov.au and they matched (apixaban 5 mg $53.79, levetiracetam 500 mg $26.83). The patient pays $25 (or $7.70 with a concession card) and the PBS pays the rest.
    • Public hospital (admissions, ED, clinics): IHACPA price weight × $7,418. This already covers doctors, nurses and medicines in hospital.

    What's not included

    Allied health, ambulance, private hospital costs, non-PBS medicines, the PBS Safety Net, brand premiums, patients' time and travel, and most of the hospital price adjustments (except the paediatric one, which you can turn on).

    How accurate is it?

    The heart list has 52 conditions plus one device scenario, and the neurology list has 53, covering adults and children. The prices are real. The % of patients and number of times for each step are estimates based on general knowledge of Australian practice, and haven't been checked against guidelines or Medicare data yet. Each pathway shows how much of its cost depends on these estimates. Use "Copy checklist" to go through them against proper sources.
